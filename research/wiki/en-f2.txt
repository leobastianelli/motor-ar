The '''FIA Formula 2 Championship''' ('''F2''') is a second tier [[single-seater]] championship organised by the [[Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile]].<ref name="F2">{{Cite news|url=https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/headlines/2017/3/newly-renamed-f2-series-to-feature-at-10-grands-prix.html|title=Newly renamed F2 series to feature at 10 Grands Prix|work=Formula1.com|access-date=9 March 2017}}</ref> The championship was introduced in 2017, following the rebranding of the long-term [[Formula One]] feeder series [[GP2 Series|GP2]]. The series original founders were [[Flavio Briatore]] and current managing director [[Bruno Michel]]. Although it is not necessary, most current F1 drivers have previously participated in Formula 2 or GP2 before graduating to Formula One. It is the final step on the [[FIA Global Pathway]] driver development ladder.

Formula 2 is designed to create an ideal training ground for life in Formula One, and to this effect, it makes racing relatively affordable for the teams. As a [[spec series]], all teams are required to use the same chassis (designed by Italian manufacturer [[Dallara]]), engine, and tyre supplier. These are designed to mirror Formula One cars in terms of safety, aesthetics, system functionalities, performance, sustainability, and cost-effectiveness. Formula 2 mainly races on European and Middle-Eastern circuits, but has made appearances at other international race tracks such as the [[Albert Park Circuit]] in Australia, the [[Miami International Autodrome]] in the USA, and the [[Circuit Gilles Villeneuve]] in Canada.

== History ==
=== Origin and first season ===
{{see also|GP2 Series}}

In 2015, the FIA announced plans to revive the [[Formula Two]] category, following the continued success of [[GP2 Series|GP2]] and [[Formula Renault 3.5]].<ref>{{cite news |url=http://m.autosport.com/news/report.php/id/118147/fia-begins-work-on-new-formula-2 |title=FIA begins work on new Formula 2 feeder category for Formula 1 |work=autosport.com |access-date=2015-03-21 |archive-date=2016-03-03 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160303221810/http://m.autosport.com/news/report.php/id/118147/fia-begins-work-on-new-formula-2 |url-status=dead}}</ref> This decision came despite the discontinuation of the [[FIA Formula Two Championship (2009–2012)|FIA Formula Two Championship]] only three years prior, owing to a lack of entries in its final season.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Elizalde |first1=Pablo |title=MotorSport Vision cancels Formula 2 championship |url=http://www.autosport.com/news/report.php/id/104751 |work=Autosport |publisher=Haymarket Media |access-date=24 January 2015 |date=6 December 2012}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.auto123.com/en/racing-news/f2-no-formula-2-championship-in-2013?artid=150678 |title=F2: No Formula 2 Championship in 2013 |work=Auto123 |first=Emmanuel |last=Rolland |date=7 December 2012 |access-date=14 September 2024}}</ref> On 8 March 2017, the FIA confirmed that the GP2 Series would be rebranded as the FIA Formula 2 Championship after an agreement with [[Liberty Media]], which had acquired both the series and the [[Formula One Group]] earlier that year.<ref>{{cite news |title=Bernie Ecclestone removed as Liberty Media completes $8bn takeover |work=[[BBC Sport]] |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/formula1/38723001 |date=23 January 2017 |access-date=23 January |access-date=30 April 2025}}</ref> FIA President at the time, [[Jean Todt]], stated that "rationalising the pathway to Formula One has been a major goal of the FIA in my time here."<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.reuters.com/article/motor-f1-series-idINL3N1GL40F/ |title=Liberty Media, FIA set to rebrand GP2 as Formula Two |website=Reuters |first=Alan |last=Baldwin |date=8 March 2017 |access-date=14 September 2024}}</ref>

The [[2017 Formula 2 Championship]] comprised eleven rounds, ten of which supported the Formula One World Championship, with an additional stand-alone event at the [[Circuito de Jerez]].<ref name="F2"/> The series retained the [[Dallara GP2/11]] chassis, in use since [[2011 GP2 Series|2011]], and the [[Mecachrome V8108 GP2 V8]], first introduced in [[2005 GP2 Series|2005]]. Rookie and reigning [[GP3 Series]] champion [[Charles Leclerc]] won the Drivers' Championship,<ref>{{Cite news |last=Blow |first=Anna |date=19 September 2020 |title=Charles Leclerc |url=https://www.insidef2.com/f2-graduates-charles-leclerc/ |access-date=28 December 2021 |work=Inside F2 |language=en-GB}}</ref> while [[Russian Time]] secured the Teams' Championship and they were the last to lift a trophy with the [[Dallara GP2/11]].<ref>{{cite news |last=Allen |first=Peter |date=26 November 2017 |title=Charles Leclerc snatches last-lap victory in final F2 race |url=https://formulascout.com/charles-leclerc-snatches-last-lap-victory-in-final-f2-race/39071 |work=Formula Scout |access-date=17 October 2025}}</ref>

=== 2018–2020 ===
[[File:FIA F2 Austria 2018 Nr. 19 Norris (1) (cropped).jpg|thumb|280px|The [[Dallara F2 2018]] added a halo crash-protection device around the head of drivers.]]
The {{F2|2018}} season introduced a new car, the [[Dallara F2 2018]], featuring the [[Mecachrome V634 engine]] and the [[Halo (safety device)|halo]] driver crash-protection device, which was also adopted by Formula One that year. [[Circuit Paul Ricard]] and the [[Sochi Autodrom]] were added to the calendar, while the Circuito de Jerez was removed. [[Carlin Motorsport|Carlin]] returned to the series after a one-year absence, and [[Charouz Racing System]] joined following the discontinuation of World Series Formula V8 3.5. Soon after the season concluded, Russian Time was sold to [[Virtuosi Racing]], which had been operating the team since [[2015 GP2 Series|2015]].<ref>{{cite news |last=Benyon |first=Jack |title=Russian Time sells Formula 2 team |url=https://www.motorsport.com/au/fia-f2/news/russian-time-formula-2-team-sale/4303584/ |work=Motorsport.com |publisher=[[Motorsport Network]] |date=25 November 2018 |archive-date=25 November 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181125162408/https://www.motorsport.com/au/fia-f2/news/russian-time-formula-2-team-sale/4303584/ |url-status=dead |access-date=17 October 2025}}</ref>

The {{F2|2019}} season witnessed the first fatality of a driver competing in the second tier of FIA-sanctioned formula racing in 10 years when French driver [[Anthoine Hubert]] was killed during the [[2019 Spa-Francorchamps Formula 2 round|feature race at Spa-Francorchamps]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/motorsport/49537761|title=Formula 2 driver Anthoine Hubert killed in Belgium crash|website=bbc.co.uk|first=Andrew|last=Benson|date=1 September 2019|access-date=23 June 2020}}</ref> The race was [[Racing flags#Red Flag|red-flagged]] and subsequently cancelled, along with the following day's sprint race. Later that year, the FIA introduced the Anthoine Hubert Award in his memory, recognising the highest-placed rookie in the standings. [[Zhou Guanyu]] became the inaugural recipient of the same.<ref name="Formula Scout">{{cite news |last1=Allen |first1=Peter |title=F2 introduces Anthoine Hubert Award, presented to Guanyu Zhou |url=http://www.formulascout.com/f2-introduces-anthoine-hubert-award-presented-to-guanyu-zhou/57534 |access-date=12 December 2019 |work=formulascout.com |publisher=Formula Scout |date=12 December 2019}}</ref>

The {{F2|2020}} season was heavily disrupted by the [[COVID-19 pandemic]], resulting in significant postponements, cancellations, and schedule revisions. The season eventually began in July and included an appearance at the [[Mugello Circuit]] for the first, and only time.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Formula 2 2020 Result for Round 9 : Tuscany , Mugello 11-13 September 2020 |url=https://www.fiaformula2.com/ |access-date=2026-03-26 |website=FIA_F2® - The Official F2® Website |language=en}}</ref>

=== 2021–2023 ===
[[File:2021 British Grand Prix (51348570357).jpg|thumb|left|The 2021 season saw numerous changes to the championship's format in an effort to cut costs, including the removal of numerous circuits that FIA Formula 3 raced on.]]
The {{F2|2021}} season underwent major restructuring as part of post-pandemic cost-cutting measures. Each round featured three races instead of two, while the number of circuits was reduced from twelve to eight. The Dallara F2 2018 chassis remained in use, despite plans for its replacement that year.<ref>{{Cite news |url=http://www.fiaformula2.com/Latest/fpa01jHYRHUQK67JSScPi/fia-formula-2-and-fia-formula-3-announce-cost-cutting-measures-for-2021-f2 |title=FIA Formula 2 and FIA Formula 3 announce cost cutting measures for 2021 onwards |work=FIA Formula 2 Championship |language=en |access-date=6 November 2020}}</ref> The calendar was separated from that of the FIA Formula 3 Championship, meaning the two series no longer ran on the same Grand Prix weekends.{{efn|The final round of the [[2021 FIA Formula 3 Championship]], was initially set to take place at the [[Circuit of the Americas]] before moving to the [[Sochi Autodrom]].}} As a result, the Red Bull Ring, Hungaroring, Circuit de Barcelona-Catalunya, Circuit Paul Ricard, and Circuit de Spa-Francorchamps were no longer included on the calendar, while the Jeddah Corniche Circuit was added to the schedule.<ref>{{Cite news|title=Updated FIA Formula 2 2021 Championship provisional calendar|url=https://www.fiaformula2.com/Latest/3ppB4LnZQW4V9J4P6rbZUc/updated-fia-formula-2-2021-championship-provisional-calendar|access-date=12 January 2021|website=FIA_F2 - The Official F2 Website|language=en}}</ref>

The {{F2|2022}} championship marked a return to the pre-2021 format of two races per round,{{efn|The two-race format was altered; the sprint race now took place before the feature race with its starting grid based on the reversal of the top ten in qualifying.}} alongside a reduction in points awarded for sprint races, pole positions, and fastest laps. The calendar expanded to twenty-eight races across fourteen rounds. Circuits dropped in 2021 returned to the schedule, alongside the addition of two venues—[[Imola Circuit]] and [[Circuit Zandvoort]]. The round at Sochi Autodrom was cancelled following the [[Russian invasion of Ukraine]]. In {{F2|2023}}, Formula 2 raced at the [[Albert Park Circuit]] for the first time. Although Imola was initially scheduled, the round was cancelled due to [[2023 Emilia-Romagna floods|severe flooding in the area]].

=== 2024–present ===
[[File:FIA F2 Austria 2024 Nr. 12 Colapinto.jpg|thumb|280px|The [[Dallara F2 2024]] was introduced to better accommodate female drivers and better resemble the 2022 Formula One regulation cycle.]]

A new chassis—the [[Dallara F2 2024]]—was introduced in {{F2|2024}}. Designed to more closely resemble the current generation of Formula One cars, it incorporated advanced safety technologies<ref>{{cite news |title=FIA Formula 2 next generation car breaks cover in Monza |url=https://www.fiaformula2.com/Latest/6L7YrcVR1WQ7QCY5HPKW7r/fia-formula-2-next-generation-car-breaks-cover-in-monza |work=FIA Formula 2 |access-date=9 February 2024 |language=en |date=31 August 2023}}</ref> and was engineered to better accommodate female drivers.<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.racefans.net/2022/12/01/formula-2-recognises-need-for-new-2024-chassis-to-suit-women-racers-better |title=F2 will make sure new 2024 car "is not going to be an issue for female drivers" |work=Race Fans |first=Ida |last=Wood |date=1 December 2022 |access-date=4 August 2023}}</ref> The series also made its debut at the [[Lusail International Circuit]], which replaced the round at Zandvoort on the calendar.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.autosport.com/formula2/news/f2-swaps-dutch-for-qatar-round-as-2024-calendar-revealed/10504215/|title=F2 swaps Dutch for Qatar round as 2024 calendar revealed|website=autosport.com|first=Haydn|last=Cobb|date=4 August 2023|access-date=4 August 2023}}</ref>

The [[Imola Circuit]] was removed from the calendar following {{F2|2025}} and will be replaced by the [[Madring]] from {{F2|2026}} onwards.<ref name="circuits">{{cite news |title=Formula 2 and Formula 3 unveil their 2026 calendars |url=https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/formula-2-and-formula-3-unveil-their-2026-calendars.4XZVkowTI7nQy17Fy7a4ze |website=Formula One |access-date=25 August 2025 |language=en}}</ref>

== Race weekend ==
A Formula 2 race weekend is structured over three days. It typically begins on Friday with a 45-minute practice session followed by a 30-minute qualifying session. The first race, known as the sprint race, is held on Saturday. Its grid is formed by reversing the top ten positions from qualifying, and the race is run over a distance of approximately {{convert|120|km|mi|abbr=on}}{{efn|The [[Monte Carlo Formula 2 round]] is run for {{convert|100|km|mi|abbr=on}} instead.}} or a maximum duration of 45 minutes, whichever is reached first. The weekend concludes on Sunday with the feature race, traditionally the main event, which precedes the [[Formula 1 Grand Prix]]. This race covers around {{convert|170|km|mi|abbr=on}}{{efn|The [[Monte Carlo Formula 2 round]] is run to {{convert|140|km|mi|abbr=on}} kilometres and the [[Budapest Formula 2 round]] is run to {{convert|160|km|mi|abbr=on}} kilometres instead.}} or lasts up to one hour, depending on which limit is reached first. Between [[2017 FIA Formula 2 Championship|2017]] and [[2020 FIA Formula 2 Championship|2020]], the feature race was scheduled on Saturdays, with the sprint race moved to Sundays.<ref>{{cite web|title=FIA Formula 2 Championship The Rules and Regulations |url=https://www.fiaformula2.com/About/DyImndAsBNFcqYOOm4yWS/the-regulations-f2 |website=FIA Formula 2}}</ref>

The driver who secures pole position in Friday's qualifying session is awarded two additional championship points. In both the sprint and feature races, one point is granted for the fastest lap, provided the driver finishes within the top ten of the final classification. The maximum number of points achievable by a single driver in one round is 39.

In the event that two or more drivers finish the season with an equal number of points, a countback system is applied to determine their standings. Priority is given to the driver with the greater number of race victories. If still tied, the number of second-place finishes is compared, followed by third-place finishes, and so forth, until the tie is resolved. This procedure applies at all stages of the championship.

==Drivers==

=== Numbering System ===
The FIA Formula 2 Championship allocates car numbers based on the results of the previous season's team standings, following a system similar to that used in Formula One between 1996 and 2013.<ref>{{cite web |title=An Introduction to Driver Numbers in Formula 1 |url=https://www.williamsf1.com/posts/b0a21eb2-dacc-4831-ab49-c5f821e28af9/an-introduction-to-driver-numbers-in-formula-1 |website=Atlassian Williams Racing |access-date=19 August 2025 |language=en}}</ref> Numbers are assigned sequentially, with the exception of number 13, which has remained unused since the introduction of the GP2 Series in 2005. In tribute to [[Anthoine Hubert]], who suffered fatal injuries in [[2019 Spa-Francorchamps Formula 2 round|Spa-Francorchamps in 2019]], the number 19 was permanently retired ahead of the 2020 season. The following year, the number 18 was also withdrawn in his memory.<ref>{{Cite news |url=https://www.motorsportweek.com/news/id/25758 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200115130632/https://www.motorsportweek.com/news/id/25758 |url-status=dead |archive-date=15 January 2020 |title=Formula 2: Formula 2 retires #19 from future use in honor of Anthoine Hubert – Motorsport Week |date=15 January 2020}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |date=27 August 2020 |title=The Star That Will Always Shine #AH19 |url=https://www.insidef2.com/the-star-that-will-always-shine/ |access-date=28 December 2021 |work=Inside F2 |language=en-GB}}</ref>

=== Drivers graduated to F1 ===
{{see also|GP2 Series#Drivers graduated to F1}}
Since its rebranding in 2017, Formula 2 has established itself as the principal feeder series to [[Formula One]], with many of its drivers progressing directly to the top tier of motorsport. [[Charles Leclerc]] and [[Sergey Sirotkin]] were the first graduate from the series to F1 following their debuts with [[Sauber]] and [[Williams F1|Williams]] in {{F1|2018}}, respectively. Leclerc entered Formula One as the reigning [[2017 Formula 2 Championship|Formula 2 champion]], while Sirotkin had made a one-round appearance in the inaugural F2 season as a reserve driver and had previously competed in [[GP2 Series|GP2]], finishing third in both the [[2015 GP2 Series|2015]] and [[2016 GP2 Series|2016]] standings. In {{F1|2019}}, the three leading drivers from the [[2018 Formula 2 Championship|2018 season]] graduated to Formula One: champion [[George Russell (racing driver)|George Russell]], runner-up [[Lando Norris]], and third-place finisher [[Alex Albon]], who debuted with Williams, [[McLaren]], and [[Scuderia Toro Rosso]], respectively. The following [[2020 Formula One World Championship|year]], {{F2|2019}} runner-up [[Nicholas Latifi]] joined Williams as the only full-time newcomer from Formula 2, while [[Jack Aitken]], who finished fifth in the 2019 standings, made a single appearance at the {{F1 GP|2020|Sakhir}} as Williams' reserve driver.{{Citation needed|date=August 2025}}

The {{F1|2021}} Formula One grid featured three graduates from the [[2020 FIA Formula 2 Championship|2020 season]]: champion [[Mick Schumacher]] and fifth-placed [[Nikita Mazepin]] joined [[Haas F1 Team|Haas]], while third-placed [[Yuki Tsunoda]] debuted with [[Scuderia AlphaTauri|AlphaTauri]]. [[Zhou Guanyu]], who finished third in the [[2021 Formula 2 Championship]] standings, was the only full-time graduate in {{F1|2022}}, signing with [[Alfa Romeo in Formula One|Alfa Romeo]]. That season also saw the debut of 2019 champion [[Nyck de Vries]], who substituted for Williams at the {{F1 GP|2022|Italian}}. De Vries had since raced in the [[FIA World Endurance Championship]] and [[Formula E]], where he won the 2021 title, before joining AlphaTauri full-time the next [[2023 Formula One Championship|year]]. In 2023, two more former F2 drivers made their Formula One debuts: [[Oscar Piastri]], 2021's Formula 2 champion (who also served as [[Alpine F1 Team|Alpine]]'s reserve driver in 2022) and [[2022 Formula 2 Championship]] fourth-place finisher [[Logan Sargeant]], who signed with McLaren and Williams, respectively.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Logan Sargeant – Formula 2 |date=12 February 2023 |url=https://www.insidef2.com/logan-sargeant/ |access-date=2024-02-04 |language=en-GB}}</ref> The 2023 season also saw the debut of [[Liam Lawson]], who finished third in Formula 2 in 2022 and made reserve driver appearances for AlphaTauri whilst also competing in [[Super Formula]]. He would later gain a full-time seat with the team during {{F1|2024}}. Three other drivers also made in-season debuts in 2024. [[Oliver Bearman]], sixth in the {{F2|2023}} standings, raced for both [[Scuderia Ferrari|Ferrari]] and Haas as a reserve driver before securing a full-time Haas seat in {{F1|2025}}. [[Franco Colapinto]] left his [[2024 Formula 2 Championship|2024 Formula 2 campaign]] mid-season to join Williams, while [[Jack Doohan]], third in 2023, debuted for Alpine in the final race of the year ahead of a full-time promotion in {{F1|2025}}. The 2025 season featured three additional graduates: 2024 champion [[Gabriel Bortoleto]] joined Sauber, runner-up [[Isack Hadjar]] signed with [[Racing Bulls]], and sixth-placed [[Kimi Antonelli]] entered Formula One with [[Mercedes F1 Team|Mercedes]].{{Citation needed|date=August 2025}}

For {{F1|2026}}, sixth-placed [[Arvid Lindblad]] has joined [[Racing Bulls]].

In 2025, Lando Norris became the first graduate to win a [[List of Formula One World Drivers' Champions|Formula One World Drivers' Championship]].<ref>{{Cite tweet |number=1997681665058234517 |user=Formula2 |title=LANDO NORRIS IS AN @F1 WORLD CHAMPION!!! 🏆He becomes the first FIA Formula 2 graduate to take the Formula 1 crown 👑👏#F2 #RoadToF1 #AbuDhabiGP |date=7 December 2025 |access-date=8 December 2025}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Benson |first=Andrew |date=7 December 2025 |title=Lando Norris wins F1 drivers' title in Abu Dhabi Grand Prix won by Max Verstappen |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/formula1/articles/czrk61y2nyjo |access-date=8 December 2025 |website=[[BBC Sport]] |language=}}</ref>

===Anthoine Hubert Award===

== Car, technology and cost ==

Running a Formula 2 car is estimated to cost around US$3 million per season.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Rencken |first=Dieter |date=28 July 2021 |title=Is F2's $3 million admission price good value for aspiring F1 drivers? |url=https://www.racefans.net/2021/07/28/is-formula-2s-3-million-admission-price-good-value-for-aspiring-f1-drivers/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220508065137/https://www.racefans.net/2021/07/28/is-formula-2s-3-million-admission-price-good-value-for-aspiring-f1-drivers/ |archive-date=8 May 2022|access-date=8 May 2022|website=Racefans.net}}</ref> In most cases, drivers must cover these expenses themselves, either through sponsorships or personal and family resources. These high costs remain one of the principal barriers to progressing into Formula One. To ease the financial burden, the FIA has implemented a number of cost-control measures, including freezing car specifications and reducing the number of venues, most notably with changes introduced for the 2021 season.<ref>{{Cite web |title=FIA Formula 2 and FIA Formula 3 announce cost-cutting measures for 2021 onwards |url=https://www.fiaformula2.com/Latest/fpa01jHYRHUQK67JSScPi/fia-formula-2-and-fia-formula-3-announce-cost-cutting-measures-for-2021-f2 |access-date=8 May 2022 |website=FIA_F2® – The Official F2® Website |language=en}}</ref>

{{As of|2025}}, all teams in the FIA Formula 2 Championship compete with an [[Dallara F2 2024|identical car]] built around a carbon-fiber [[Dallara]] chassis and powered by a [[turbocharger|single-turbocharged]] [[Mecachrome V634 engine]], which is next scheduled for an update in 2029. The car is expected to weigh a minimum of {{convert|755|kg|lb|0|abbr=on}}, with the driver, and is equipped with a [[Hewland]]-supplied gearbox featuring an eight-position barrel with ratchet body and software refinements that improve gear selection.{{citation needed|date=August 2025}} Clutches are supplied by [[ZF Sachs]] and operated through a hand-paddle lever.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.fiaformula2.com/About/14LCsEEMG9yyx5DkhcN1J8/the-car-and-engine-f2|title=Formula 2|website=FIA_F2® – The Official F2® Website}}</ref>

Since the inception of the [[GP2 Series]] in 2005, the [[OZ Group]] has been the sole wheel supplier. In 2020, the category moved from {{convert|13|in|mm|0|abbr=on}} to {{convert|18|in|mm|0|abbr=on}} magnesium-alloy wheels inspired by road-car designs. This change, made in anticipation of Formula One's 2022 wheel regulations, also allowed closer data sharing between the two series.<ref>{{cite web|title=Formula 2 cars to use 18-inch Pirelli tyres from 2020|url=https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article.formula-2-cars-to-use-18-inch-pirelli-tyres-from-2020.7FFg3gk2eKzsIwUdZerdvk.html|work=Formula1.com|publisher=[[Formula One|Formula 1]]|date=9 May 2019|access-date=9 May 2019}}</ref> [[Pirelli]] has exclusively supplied tyres for Formula 2 since 2017. They currently provide four slick options: a purple supersoft, red soft, yellow medium, and white hard. A single wet compound with a blue sidewall is also supplied during rainy conditions.<ref>{{Cite web |title=SOFTEST TYRES IN THE RANGE FOR THE F2 AND F3 DRIVERS IN AUSTRIA |url=https://press.pirelli.com/softest-tyres-in-the-range-for-the-f2-and-f3-drivers-in-austria/ |access-date=2026-03-26 |website=SOFTEST TYRES IN THE RANGE FOR THE F2 AND F3 DRIVERS IN AUSTRIA |language=en}}</ref>

The cars are equipped with a standard FIA Premier FT5 safety tank with a maximum capacity of 125 litres. Refuelling during races, however, is prohibited for safety and cost control reasons. In 2023, [[Aramco]] became the official fuel partner for the series, providing all entrants with standardised fuel.<ref>{{cite web|title=Formula 2 and Formula 3 partner with Aramco to pioneer low-carbon fuels from 2023|url=https://www.aramco.com/en/news-media/news/2022/formula-2-and-formula-3-partner-with-aramco-to-pioneer-low-carbon-fuels-from-2023|last=Dhahran|work=aramco.com|publisher=[[Aramco]]|date=2 September 2022|access-date=2 September 2022}}</ref>

[[File: 2024 FIA Formula 2 Silverstone (54042418131) (cropped).jpg|thumb|280px|[[Kimi Antonelli]] driving the [[Dallara F2 2024]] at [[Silverstone Circuit]] for [[Prema Racing]].]]

Driver control systems remain deliberately simple. Steering is rack-and-pinion and entirely manual, without power assistance, similar to the [[IndyCar Series]]. XAP Technology has supplied steering wheels since 2011, with the current Formula 2451 S3 model introduced in 2018. It includes six front-facing buttons, five rear paddles for DRS, gear shifting, and clutch operation, as well as three rotary switches, closely resembling the layout of its [[Formula E]] counterpart. Electronics are also standardised across the grid: all cars carry a Marelli Marvel SRG 480 [[electronic control unit]] and a Marelli PDU 12-42 power supply management unit. [[Telemetry#Motor racing|Telemetry]] is limited to broadcast purposes, though teams may access data when the car is in the garage.<ref>{{Cite web |title=FIA Formula One World Championship regulations |url=https://www.fia.com/regulation/category/110 |access-date=2026-03-26 |website=Federation Internationale de l'Automobile |language=en}}</ref>

Since 2015, the series has also employed the [[Drag reduction system|Drag Reduction System]] (DRS), which lowers the angle of the rear wing's upper element by more than 40 degrees to aid overtaking when a driver is within one second of a rival. The system is deactivated automatically in wet conditions for safety.<ref>{{Cite news |title=GP2 Series to introduce Drag Reduction System from 2015 |url=https://www.autosport.com/formula2/news/gp2-series-to-introduce-drag-reduction-system-from-2015-5046823/5046823/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250120083017/https://www.autosport.com/formula2/news/gp2-series-to-introduce-drag-reduction-system-from-2015-5046823/5046823/ |archive-date=2025-01-20 |access-date=2026-03-26 |language=en}}</ref>

Despite being slower than [[Formula One cars]], Formula 2 remains amongst the fastest single-seaters in motorsport, capable of reaching top speeds of around 335&nbsp;km/h in low-downforce configuration. Their distinguishing performance lies in cornering and braking, where downforce allows up to 3.9 g of lateral acceleration and 3.6 g under braking,<ref>{{Cite web |title=Formula 2 |url=https://www.fiaformula2.com/ |access-date=2024-06-06 |website=FIA_F2® - The Official F2® Website |language=en}}</ref>  compared with 2.6 g for [[FIA Formula 3 Championship|Formula 3]]<ref>{{Cite web |title=Formula 3 |url=https://www.fiaformula3.com/ |access-date=2024-06-06 |website=FIAFormula3® - The Official F3® Website |language=en}}</ref> and around 1.7 g for production-based [[Porsche Carrera Cup]] cars.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Morrison |first=Mac |date=10 July 2023 |title=Supercar vs. Race Car Test: 2023 Porsche 911 GT3 RS vs. 911 GT3 Cup at Road America! |url=https://www.motortrend.com/reviews/2023-porsche-911-gt3-rs-vs-gt3-cup-race-car-comparison-test/ |access-date=6 June 2024 |website=[[Motor Trend]]}}</ref> As an example of the differences between the FIA formula categories, at [[Albert Park Circuit]], the Formula 2 lap record stands at 1:28.989,<ref>{{Cite web |title=Formula 2 2024 Result for Round 3 : Australia, Melbourne 22-24 March 2024 |url=https://www.fiaformula2.com/ |access-date=2024-06-06 |website=FIA_F2® - The Official F2® Website |language=en}}</ref> compared with 1:19.813 for Formula One<ref>{{Cite web |title=Australian Grand Prix 2024 - F1 Race |url=https://www.formula1.com/en/racing/2024/Australia/Circuit.html |access-date=2024-06-06 |website=Formula 1® - The Official F1® Website |language=en}}</ref> and 1:33.025 for Formula 3.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Formula 3 2024 Result for Round 2 : Australia, Melbourne 22-24 March 2024 |url=https://www.fiaformula3.com/ |access-date=2024-06-06 |website=FIAFormula3® - The Official F3® Website |language=en}}</ref> As [[Liam Lawson]], a driver with experience across F1, F2, and Japan's Super Formula, noted in 2023, the latter category is "faster than Formula 2, a lot closer to Formula 1, and sometimes it even feels like F1 in some corners. Downforce is pretty exceptional."<ref>{{Cite web |last=Timothy |first=Neal |date=11 April 2023 |title=Lawson quickly settles into Japanese Super Formula |url=https://autoaction.com.au/2023/04/11/lawson-quickly-settles-into-japanese-super-formula |access-date=6 June 2024 |website=[[Auto Action]]}}</ref>

At the same time, speed has also been progressively reinforced over the series’ history. Each car must meet FIA standards for frontal, side, rear, and steering-column impact tests and is built with roll hoops, impact structures, and push-tested monocoques. Anti-intrusion survival cells have been mandatory since 2011, alongside wheel tether cables to reduce the risk of wheels detaching in crashes. The [[Halo (safety device)|halo]] driver crash-protection device was introduced in 2018.<ref>{{Cite web |title=F2 Explained with Alex Brundle: F2 Safety – Formula 2 |date=6 June 2022 |url=https://www.insidef2.com/f2-explained-with-alex-brundle-f2-safety/ |access-date=2024-02-04 |language=en-GB}}</ref>

== Media coverage ==
Formula Two is broadcast live in almost every country and territory worldwide. From 2017 to 2024, commentary for races was covered by Alex Jacques. In 2025, the FIA announced that Chris McCarthey would be replacing him going forward.<ref>{{cite web |date=6 December 2024 |title="This Will Be My Final Year": Alex Jacques Announces Retirement From F2 Commentary After 10 Years |url=https://thesportsrush.com/f2-news-this-will-be-my-final-year-alex-jacques-announces-retirement-from-f2-commentary-after-10-years/ |access-date=26 March 2026 |website=The Sports Rush}}</ref>

Although Formula One Management owns the television rights for this series, alongside those of Formula One, FIA Formula 3 Championship and F1 Academy, they have licensed the same to several different regional companies. The series is also available globally through [[F1 TV]], Formula One Management's own streaming platform.<ref>{{cite web |title=Where to watch F2? |url=https://www.fiaformula2.com/About/5KKoOKrH2MbJlUgtRMYDQy/where-to-watch-us-f2 |access-date=26 March 2026 |website=FIA Formula 2 Championship}}</ref> Sky-branded operators hold rights in several territories: [[Sky Sports]] in the United Kingdom, [[Sky Deutschland]] in Germany, and [[Sky Sport (New Zealand)|Sky Sport]] in New Zealand, with [[Sky Italia]] covering Italy. [[Viaplay]] holds rights across Denmark, Norway, Finland, Iceland, and the Netherlands, while Sweden is served by V Sport and Viaplay Channels. In Africa, [[SuperSport (South Africa)|SuperSport]] is the primary broadcaster, while [[beIN Sports]] covers the Middle East, North Africa, Turkey, and much of Asia. In North America, coverage in Canada is provided by [[RDS (Canadian TV channel)|RDS]] and [[TSN (Canadian TV network)|TSN]], while United States rights are held by [[Apple TV+]] as part of its Formula One broadcast agreement.<ref>{{cite web |title=Where to watch F2? |url=https://www.fiaformula2.com/About/5KKoOKrH2MbJlUgtRMYDQy/where-to-watch-us-f2 |access-date=26 March 2026 |website=FIA Formula 2 Championship}}</ref>

Between 2019 and 2021, [[Match! Arena]] broadcast races in Russia. Following Russia's invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, Formula One Management terminated its Russian broadcast contracts across all series under its control, ending coverage in March 2022.<ref>{{cite web |date=10 March 2022 |title=F1 terminates Russian TV contracts, ends F1 TV availability |url=https://www.motorsport.com/f1/news/f1-terminates-russian-tv-contracts-ends-f1-tv-availability/8873427/ |access-date=26 March 2026 |website=Motorsport.com}}</ref>
