The '''NASCAR Cup Series Drivers' Championship''' is awarded by the chairman of [[NASCAR]] to the most successful [[NASCAR Cup Series]] racing car driver over a season, as determined by a [[NASCAR rules and regulations#Championship points system|points system]] based on race results. The Drivers' Championship was first awarded in [[1949 in NASCAR|1949]] to [[Red Byron]].<ref name="byron">{{cite web|title=NHOF: New Class Unveiled Tonight |url=http://nascar.speedtv.com/article/nhof-new-nascar-hall-of-fame-class-unveiled-tonight/ |publisher=[[Fox Sports]] |work=[[Speed (TV channel)|Speed]] |access-date=23 June 2012 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120525003732/http://nascar.speedtv.com/article/nhof-new-nascar-hall-of-fame-class-unveiled-tonight/ |archive-date=25 May 2012 }}</ref> The first driver to win multiple Championships was [[Herb Thomas]] in [[1951 in NASCAR|1951]] and [[1953 in NASCAR|1953]]. The current Drivers' Champion is [[Kyle Larson]], who won his second NASCAR Cup Series championship in [[2025 NASCAR Cup Series|2025]].<ref name="8WChamps">{{cite web |last=Diepraam |first=Mattijs |last2=Galpin |first2=Darren |last3=Young |first3=Rob |last4=Korzan |first4=Nicolás |last5=Costa |first5=Paulo |last6=Ullrich |first6=Andreas |last7=Jones |first7=Mark Alan |date=December 11, 2022 |title=Lower category champions – NASCAR Winston Cup |url=https://www.forix.com/8w/6thgear/champs.html#NASCAR |access-date=July 6, 2023 |work=8W |publisher=Forix}}</ref>

The NASCAR points system has undergone several incarnations since its initial implementation. Originally, races awarded points by a complicated system based upon final positioning and weighted by prize money purses, such that higher-paying events gave more points. Soon after the advent of the modern era in 1972, the championship was decided by a more basic cumulative point total based solely upon a driver's finishing position in each race. In order to reduce the possibility of a driver clinching before the final event, NASCAR implemented the "[[NASCAR Chase|Chase for the Cup]]" in 2004 which, with minor modifications from 2004 to 2013,<ref name="pointsoldandnew">{{cite news | url=http://usatoday30.usatoday.com/sports/motor/nascar/2004-01-21-pearson-points_x.htm | title=David Pearson endorses new point system | work=[[USA Today]] | date=21 January 2004 | access-date=22 June 2013 | author=Graves, Gary}}</ref> more radical changes in 2014<ref name="2014 Chase changes">{{cite press release|url=http://www.nascar.com/en_us/news-media/articles/2014/1/30/chase-for-the-nascar-sprint-cup-championship-format-changes.html |title=NASCAR Announces Chase for the Sprint Cup Format Change |publisher=NASCAR |date=January 30, 2014 |access-date=January 30, 2014}}</ref> and a simpler revamp in 2026,<ref name="2026 Chase return">{{cite web |url=https://www.nascar.com/news-media/2026/01/12/nascar-returns-to-chase-championship-format-for-2026/ |title=NASCAR returns to Chase championship format for 2026 |website=[[NASCAR]] |date=January 12, 2026 |access-date=January 12, 2026 }}</ref> stands as the current format. Before the final ten races, the top 16 drivers, based exclusively on points, have their points reset to an amount based on their position prior to the reset.<ref name="2026 Chase return"/>  With these changes, the last Drivers' Champion to clinch before the final race was [[Matt Kenseth]] in [[2003 NASCAR Winston Cup Series|2003]].<ref>{{cite web | url=http://www.motortrend.com/auto_news/112_news_031111_nascar/ | title=Matt Kenseth Claims NASCAR Winston Cup Championship | work=[[Motor Trend]] | date=10 November 2003 | access-date=22 June 2013}}</ref>

Overall, thirty-six different drivers have won the Championship,<ref name="8WChamps" /> with [[Richard Petty]],<ref name="petty">{{cite web|title=Richard Petty Information |url=http://www.nascar.com/news/features/by.the.numbers.petty.enterprises/index.html |publisher=[[Turner Sports]] |work=[[NASCAR]] |access-date=22 June 2010 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110525085521/http://www.nascar.com/news/features/by.the.numbers.petty.enterprises/index.html |archive-date=25 May 2011 |url-status=dead }}</ref> [[Dale Earnhardt]],<ref name="earnhardt">{{cite news|title=Earnhardt Information|url=http://sportsillustrated.cnn.com/statitudes/news/2001/02/18/earnhardt_btn/index.html|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20010912210613/http://sportsillustrated.cnn.com/statitudes/news/2001/02/18/earnhardt_btn/index.html|url-status=dead|archive-date=September 12, 2001|publisher=CNN Sports|work=[[Sports Illustrated]]|access-date=22 June 2010}}</ref> and [[Jimmie Johnson]] holding the record for most titles at seven. Johnson has the record for most consecutive Drivers' Championships, winning five from [[2006 NASCAR Nextel Cup Series|2006]] to [[2010 NASCAR Sprint Cup Series|2010]].<ref name="johnson">{{cite news|title=Jimmie Johnson wins fifth straight NASCAR Sprint Cup championship|url=http://www.nydailynews.com/sports/more_sports/2010/11/21/2010-11-21_jimmie_johnson_wins_fifth_straight_nascar_sprint_cup_championship_.html|newspaper=[[New York Daily News]]|access-date=13 June 2011 | location=Homestead, Florida | first=Richard | last=Biebrich | date=21 November 2010}}</ref>  Thus far, every champion has originated from the United States.<ref name="8WChamps" /> Byron has the fewest number of race starts before winning his first title with six in 1949.<ref>{{Cite web |date=November 2, 2022 |title=Fewest starts before first NASCAR Cup championship |url=https://www.nascar.com/gallery/fewest-starts-by-driver-before-first-nascar-cup-championship/ |access-date=September 13, 2024 |publisher=[[NASCAR]]}}</ref> [[Bill Rexford]] is the youngest Cup Series champion; he was 23 years, 7 months, and 15 days old when he won the title in [[1950 NASCAR Grand National Series|1950]].<ref>{{Cite web |date=November 11, 2021 |title=Drivers under 30 to win a NASCAR Cup Series title |url=https://www.nascar.com/gallery/youngest-drivers-to-win-a-nascar-cup-series-championship/ |access-date=May 8, 2023 |website=NASCAR}}</ref> [[Bobby Allison]] is the oldest Cup Series champion; he was 45 years, 11 months, and 17 days old when he won the championship in [[1983 NASCAR Winston Cup Series|1983]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Jensen |first=Tom |date=January 12, 2017 |title=45 days to Daytona: Allison, Petty oldest champions at age 45 |url=http://www.foxsports.com/nascar/story/45-days-daytona-allison-petty-oldest-champions-age-45-011217 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170113210445/http://www.foxsports.com/nascar/story/45-days-daytona-allison-petty-oldest-champions-age-45-011217 |archive-date=January 13, 2017 |access-date=May 8, 2023 |website=[[Fox Sports (United States)|Fox Sports]]}}</ref>
